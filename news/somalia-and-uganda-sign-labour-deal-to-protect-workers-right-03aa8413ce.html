Somalia and Uganda Sign Labour Deal to Protect Workers' Rights - Dawan Africa

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 14:49:50 GMT

Somalia and Uganda have signed a bilateral labour agreement aimed at safeguarding workers' rights and regulating employment practices. The agreement was finalized during the Somalia-Uganda Joint Permanent Commission mid-term review, business forum, and joint exhibition held in Mogadishu from October 20 to 22, 2026. Uganda's Labour Minister, Henry Tumukunde, highlighted that the deal addresses issues faced by Ugandan workers in Somalia, such as unpaid wages, withheld passports, and lack of written contracts. Additionally, the two nations agreed on a revised Bilateral Air Services Agreement, which will undergo legal review before being signed in December 2026. Plans for military training and education agreements are also in place, with a joint defense committee expected to be established by the end of December. Furthermore, both countries aim to finalize the creation of a Joint Permanent Council on Investment and Trade by November 30. The meeting concluded with commitments to annual reviews of bilateral agreements, with the next session scheduled for 2027.
