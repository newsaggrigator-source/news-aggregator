Plundering the Seas: How Corruption and Secret Foreign Deals Dismantled Somalia’s Blue Economy - Garowe Online

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 06:55:41 GMT

The recent 3rd Somali Fisheries Week in Mogadishu highlighted the severe decline of Somalia's blue economy, marked by corruption and foreign exploitation. Initially a promising event, it was reduced to a mere two-day gathering lacking international support and legitimacy, reflecting the ministry's isolation. Investigations into the previous year's event revealed significant financial mismanagement, leading to the withdrawal of key Western donors like USAID and the UK's FCDO, which had provided essential funding for fisheries programs. In response, the Ministry of Fisheries secured a controversial multimillion-dollar deal with Turkey, allowing foreign fleets access to Somalia's Exclusive Economic Zone, raising concerns about the loss of national resources. Additionally, the ministry's partnership with the private Hope Labs Group to manage regulatory affairs bypassed legal protocols, further jeopardizing local fishers and the ecosystem. Without urgent oversight and anti-corruption measures, Somalia risks permanently losing control over its vital marine resources.
