Egypt, Sudan, Eritrea Reaffirm Coastal States’ Role in Red Sea Security at Aswan - FTL Somalia

Sun, 22 Nov 2026 14:05:47 GMT

At a trilateral meeting in Aswan, Egypt, Sudan, and Eritrea's foreign ministers reaffirmed the exclusive responsibility of coastal states for governing the Red Sea and ensuring its security. This meeting, part of the sixth Aswan Forum for Sustainable Peace and Development, followed a previous summit in New Alamein that included Somalia. The ministers rejected unilateral actions regarding security and water issues, particularly concerning the Nile, emphasizing adherence to international law. They also expressed support for Somalia's sovereignty and territorial integrity, explicitly opposing any recognition of Somaliland. The discussions highlighted the significance of littoral states in regional governance, especially in light of Ethiopia's ambitions for Red Sea access. The meeting underscored the complexities of regional alliances, as the bloc's coherence relies on shared opposition to Ethiopia, which remains excluded from these consultations. The Aswan Forum's outcomes raise questions about the potential for stability in the region amidst ongoing tensions. (160 words)
