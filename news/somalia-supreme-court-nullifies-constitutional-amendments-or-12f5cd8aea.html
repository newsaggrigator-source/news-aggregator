Somalia supreme court nullifies constitutional amendments, orders elections within 90 days - Somali Guardian

Thu, 05 Nov 2026 17:13:10 GMT

Somalia’s Supreme Court has declared the amendments to the 2012 Provisional Constitution null and void, invalidating the extensions of the presidential and parliamentary mandates. The court ordered that elections be held within 90 days, following a ruling that deemed the amendments unlawful, as they were ratified after the parliament's constitutional mandate had expired. This ruling came shortly after President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud dismissed Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf, seemingly to prevent a judicial decision that could challenge his authority to remain in office beyond his term, which expired in May. The court emphasized that the current administration is functioning in a caretaker capacity and lacks the authority for significant policy decisions. Following the verdict, security forces stormed the Supreme Court, leading to fears of reprisals against the judges involved. The opposition welcomed the ruling and condemned the government's actions, while the dismissed chief justice and the judges sought refuge with former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed.
