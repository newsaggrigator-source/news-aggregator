UN political mission in Somalia closes after decades of support - Horseed Media

Sat, 31 Oct 2026 15:35:43 GMT

The United Nations has officially closed its political mission in Somalia, concluding decades of support that began during a period of civil war and state collapse in the early 1990s. The UN Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) ended its operations, with officials noting significant progress in establishing national and federal institutions, despite ongoing challenges. UN Deputy Special Representative Adam Abdelmoula Zenenga highlighted advancements in governance, political inclusion, and human rights frameworks. However, he emphasized the need for continued international support to sustain these gains. While the mission's closure transfers responsibilities to Somali institutions, approximately 25 UN agencies will remain active in the country, providing humanitarian and development assistance. Somalia continues to face a severe humanitarian crisis, with 4.8 million people requiring aid, exacerbated by conflict and climate-related shocks. The UN's departure marks a transition rather than a complete withdrawal, as it aims to support Somalia's ongoing recovery and development efforts.
