Somalia's escalating security crisis is deeply intertwined with political fragmentation and contested federal authority, rather than solely stemming from militant activities. The article argues that the centralization of power has exacerbated existing tensions among various political factions, leading to a decline in public trust in government institutions. As different regions vie for autonomy and influence, the lack of cohesive governance has resulted in weakened security responses to threats from militant groups. The ongoing struggles for power among political elites further complicate efforts to establish a unified front against violence and instability. The piece emphasizes that addressing these underlying political issues is crucial for any meaningful resolution to Somalia's security challenges, highlighting the need for a more inclusive and decentralized approach to governance that can foster trust and cooperation among the diverse communities within the country. Without such reforms, the cycle of violence and political discord is likely to continue.
