Ethiopia and Yemen: A Strategic Relationship Beyond Political Change - horn review

Sat, 07 Nov 2026 14:00:50 GMT

Ethiopia and Yemen have maintained a strategic relationship shaped by geographical proximity and shared interests for over a century, despite significant political changes. Their ties, formalized by a Treaty of Friendship and Commerce in 1935, have endured through various political upheavals, demonstrating that their relationship is rooted in mutual economic and maritime interests rather than solely political alignments. As Ethiopia lacks direct access to the Red Sea, it relies heavily on maritime security and regional stability for its economic growth, particularly through trade routes that pass through Yemen. Conversely, Yemen, weakened by years of conflict, sees Ethiopia as a vital market and partner for economic recovery. Both nations stand to benefit from enhanced cooperation in trade, infrastructure, and humanitarian efforts, particularly concerning migration issues. A diplomatic strategy that emphasizes continuity and practical cooperation can help both countries navigate their complex political landscapes while fostering long-term strategic interests.
