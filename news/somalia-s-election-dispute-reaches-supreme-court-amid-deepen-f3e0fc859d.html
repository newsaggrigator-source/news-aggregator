Somalia’s election dispute reaches Supreme Court amid deepening political crisis - The EastAfrican

Thu, 29 Oct 2026 12:15:00 GMT

Somalia's political crisis has intensified as opposition leaders, led by former President Sharif Sheikh Ahmed, have filed a legal challenge in the Supreme Court against recent constitutional amendments and the newly announced 2027 election timetable. The opposition, supported by the federal member states of Puntland and Jubaland, argues that the government bypassed legal procedures in revising the Provisional Constitution. Ahmed contends that the amendments lacked national consensus and should adhere to established constitutional guidelines. The Supreme Court's handling of this case could significantly impact Somalia's political landscape, as it may influence the acceptance of the upcoming electoral process by regional stakeholders. Meanwhile, former President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo has also criticized the electoral commission for its perceived bias. Amid these tensions, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud has invited opposition leaders to national consultations aimed at fostering dialogue and consensus on federal electoral issues, marking a potential shift towards resolving disputes through national institutions rather than external mediation.
