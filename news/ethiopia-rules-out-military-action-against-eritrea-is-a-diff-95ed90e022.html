Ethiopia Rules Out Military Action Against Eritrea – Is a Different Breakthrough Possible? - horn review

Thu, 26 Nov 2026 13:17:46 GMT

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has ruled out military action against Eritrea despite ongoing tensions along their border, where Ethiopian troops recently repelled a rebel attack supported by Eritrean forces. In his address to the new parliament, Abiy emphasized Ethiopia's desire for diplomatic engagement, contingent on Eritrea's cooperation regarding two key issues: mutual benefits, particularly Ethiopia's access to the sea, and Eritrea ceasing its interference in Ethiopia's internal affairs. This stance reflects a broader historical context, where Eritrea's aggressive foreign policy has led to regional instability since its secession from Ethiopia in 1993. Abiy's remarks signal a push for international intervention to de-escalate tensions, highlighting the potential for dialogue over conflict. While Ethiopia's military capabilities have grown, the prime minister asserts that a peaceful resolution is preferable, underscoring the importance of reform within Eritrea to foster better relations. The situation remains delicate, with the risk of further conflict looming.
