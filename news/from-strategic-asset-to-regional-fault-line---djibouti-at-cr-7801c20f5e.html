From Strategic Asset to Regional Fault Line - Djibouti At Crossroads of Red Sea Power Struggle

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 04:57:21 GMT

Djibouti, once a strategic asset due to its vital ports and proximity to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, is now facing significant challenges that threaten its stability and economic model. Historically reliant on Ethiopia for trade, Djibouti's position is jeopardized by Ethiopia's pursuit of alternative maritime routes, particularly through Somaliland, which could diminish Djibouti's bargaining power. The ongoing Red Sea security crisis, marked by Houthi attacks, has further complicated the situation, prompting major shipping lines to reroute, impacting Djibouti's economy. Additionally, Djibouti's heavy debt burden, exacerbated by extensive infrastructure investments funded by Chinese loans, raises concerns about its financial sustainability. Domestic unrest, fueled by high youth unemployment and political exclusion, adds to the instability, as armed groups like the FRUD-Armée challenge the government. As regional dynamics shift, Djibouti risks becoming a fault line in a broader geopolitical struggle involving multiple state and non-state actors.
