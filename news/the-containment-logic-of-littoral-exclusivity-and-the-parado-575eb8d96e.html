The Containment Logic of Littoral Exclusivity and the Paradox of Red Sea Security - horn review

Wed, 25 Nov 2026 15:26:47 GMT

The article discusses Egypt's strategy of "littoral exclusivity" in the Red Sea, which aims to limit Ethiopia's influence and access to maritime resources. This approach involves asserting that only coastal states can dictate security and political frameworks in the region, effectively sidelining landlocked Ethiopia. Egypt's efforts to contain Ethiopia include strengthening military, diplomatic, and economic ties with neighboring states like Sudan and Somalia, while simultaneously opposing Ethiopia's maritime ambitions and linking them to disputes over the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD). The article highlights the paradox of this exclusivity, as it contradicts the reality of regional interdependence and the involvement of non-littoral states in ensuring maritime security. The presence of international naval forces and the historical challenges of piracy further complicate the notion that only littoral states should govern Red Sea security. Ultimately, the rigid application of this doctrine risks exacerbating regional tensions and undermining collaborative security efforts.
