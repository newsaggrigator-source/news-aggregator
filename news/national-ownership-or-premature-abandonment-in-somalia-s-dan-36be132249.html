National Ownership or Premature Abandonment in Somalia’s Dangerous New Phase - horn review

Mon, 02 Nov 2026 18:12:08 GMT

The recent withdrawal of Ugandan troops from Lower Shabelle, coupled with Somalia's political turmoil surrounding President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's contested mandate extension, marks a critical juncture in the country's ongoing conflict with Al Shabaab. The Ugandan forces, integral to the African Union mission since 2007, have been pivotal in maintaining security in the region. Their departure, alongside the closure of the United Nations political mission, signals a dangerous shift, leaving Somalia politically fragmented and militarily vulnerable. The Somali National Army struggles to hold territory, and the political crisis undermines efforts to mobilize a unified response against Al Shabaab. As external support diminishes, the insurgency capitalizes on governance gaps and elite divisions, threatening to expand its influence. The situation underscores the precariousness of Somalia's national ownership aspirations, as the state lacks the capacity to manage its security effectively. This confluence of military withdrawal and political instability propels Somalia into a higher-risk phase of conflict.
