As if the US-Iran War Wasn’t Chaotic Enough, Pirates Are Making a Comeback - Newsweek

Thu, 19 Nov 2026 10:00:00 GMT

Piracy off the coast of Somalia has surged to levels not seen since 2013, coinciding with the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict in the Middle East. Somali pirates are exploiting a security vacuum created by the diversion of naval forces to address threats from the Iran-aligned Houthis, who have targeted vessels in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden. Experts note that the current geopolitical chaos has made piracy a lower-risk, high-reward venture, with pirates targeting ships from various nations, including the U.S. and Iran. The unrest in Somalia's Puntland region has further weakened maritime security, allowing piracy to thrive. Analysts warn that the convergence of various armed groups, including Al-Shabab and the Houthis, complicates the situation, as they may collaborate to disrupt shipping. While international naval coalitions remain active, the effectiveness of past counter-piracy efforts is uncertain, and piracy is expected to persist, adding costs to an already strained global shipping industry.
