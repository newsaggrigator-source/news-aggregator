After years of drought, flooding could now push parts of Somalia toward famine - Care.org

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 14:45:21 GMT

After enduring years of drought, Somalia faces a new crisis as forecasts predict severe flooding due to the El Niño phenomenon, potentially pushing vulnerable communities toward famine. The country narrowly avoided famine during its last major drought, but many essential health and nutrition programs have since closed due to funding shortages. With the Deyr rainy season approaching, which typically runs from October to December, experts warn that heavy rains could lead to flash floods, displacing millions and exacerbating food insecurity. Currently, 5.4 million people are projected to experience Crisis levels of hunger, with nearly 1.5 million facing Emergency conditions. The situation is further complicated by the risk of disease outbreaks linked to flooding, which can contaminate water supplies and worsen malnutrition. Humanitarian organizations, including CARE, are urging for increased funding and support to prepare for the impending floods and prevent famine, emphasizing the need for timely intervention to save lives and stabilize affected communities.
