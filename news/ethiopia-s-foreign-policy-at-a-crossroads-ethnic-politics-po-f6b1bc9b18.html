Ethiopia’s Foreign Policy at a Crossroads: Ethnic Politics, Political Patronage, and the Making of “Innocents Abroad” - Borkena

Wed, 18 Nov 2026 17:47:46 GMT

Ethiopia's foreign policy is currently challenged by internal ethnic politics and political patronage, which hinder its ability to effectively represent national interests abroad. The government faces criticism for appointing diplomats based on political connections rather than merit, raising concerns about professionalism and competence within its diplomatic corps. Recent appointments include individuals with questionable backgrounds, prompting doubts about the criteria used for selection. This practice risks creating a diplomatic service filled with "Innocents Abroad," lacking the necessary skills in international relations, law, and negotiation. Additionally, corruption and nepotism within public institutions further compromise Ethiopia's diplomatic integrity. As a strategically significant nation, Ethiopia must prioritize a coherent foreign policy and professional diplomatic representation to navigate complex international relationships, particularly regarding issues like the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam and regional tensions. Without addressing these internal challenges, Ethiopia risks damaging its international reputation and undermining its long-term strategic interests.
