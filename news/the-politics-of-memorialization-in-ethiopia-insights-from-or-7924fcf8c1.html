The Politics of Memorialization in Ethiopia: Insights from Oromia, Somali and Tigray - Rift Valley Institute

Mon, 23 Nov 2026 13:16:58 GMT

The report from the Rift Valley Institute examines the complex politics of memorialization in Ethiopia, focusing on the Oromia, Somali, and Tigray regions. It highlights how collective memories are shaped by political authority, recognition, and belonging, revealing that memory is not a fixed account but an ongoing negotiation influenced by state power and resource access. The study introduces the concept of a "selective memory regime," where political actors, including regional governments, act as "memory entrepreneurs," shaping historical narratives based on their power dynamics. The report illustrates how memorial practices, such as naming public spaces and erecting monuments, reflect shifting political landscapes and can marginalize certain histories. It emphasizes the need for democratizing memory production and creating inclusive frameworks for memorialization to address unresolved injustices. Ultimately, the report calls for dialogue and reconciliation to foster a more comprehensive understanding of Ethiopia's diverse historical narratives.
