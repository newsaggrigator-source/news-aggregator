War in Amhara, Ethiopia, is Threatening Water Security in the Nile Basin - The Water Diplomat

Thu, 26 Nov 2026 08:39:28 GMT

The ongoing war in Ethiopia's Amhara region, now in its fourth year, poses a significant threat to water security in the Nile Basin. Initiated under a military state of emergency in August 2023, the conflict has escalated, leading to devastating drone attacks and artillery bombardments on Ethiopian Orthodox Christian churches and monasteries, which are crucial for preserving the region's ecology. The Blue Nile, which originates in Amhara and is vital for Ethiopia, Sudan, and Egypt, is at risk due to the destruction of Lake Tana and its tributaries, essential for regulating water flow. The conflict has disrupted farming and conservation efforts, exacerbating ecological degradation and threatening the livelihoods of millions dependent on the Nile. Diplomatic efforts must address the conflict's implications for regional water security, emphasizing the need for immediate ceasefires, humanitarian access, and inclusive political dialogue to ensure both peace in Amhara and the long-term viability of the Nile Basin's water resources.
