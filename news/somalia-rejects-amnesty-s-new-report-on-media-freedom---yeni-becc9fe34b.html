Somalia rejects Amnesty's new report on media freedom - Yeni Safak English

Mon, 23 Nov 2026 18:41:20 GMT

Somalia's government has rejected a recent report by Amnesty International that claims media freedom has worsened since President Mohamed Abdullahi Mohamed took office in 2017. The Somali government argues that the report fails to acknowledge the progress made in protecting journalists and addressing media freedom issues over the past three years. It highlights that at least eight journalists have been killed during this period, with Amnesty stating that Somali journalists face severe threats, including violence and arbitrary arrests. In response, the Somali government insists it is committed to safeguarding individual rights and disputes the accuracy of the report, claiming that many cases cited do not pertain to media freedom. The government also denies allegations of bribery involving private media, labeling them as falsehoods intended to mislead the public. Somalia has faced significant challenges, including terrorism, particularly from the al-Qaeda-affiliated group al-Shabaab, which has contributed to a precarious environment for journalists.
