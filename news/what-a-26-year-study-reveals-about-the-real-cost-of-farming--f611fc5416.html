What a 26-Year Study Reveals About the Real Cost of Farming in Somalia - Dawan Africa

Tue, 10 Nov 2026 21:06:12 GMT

A recent 26-year study conducted by researchers from SIMAD University highlights the multifaceted challenges of farming in Somalia, emphasizing that agricultural success hinges not only on rainfall but also on security and market access. The study, published in July 2026, analyzed seven crops—maize, sorghum, cowpea, sesame, rice, banana, and lime—over the period from 2000 to 2025. It found that while favorable rainfall correlates with higher profitability, conflict significantly undermines crop returns by disrupting transport and market access. The researchers advocate for a comprehensive approach to agricultural investment that includes climate adaptation, improved infrastructure, and market information systems. They stress the importance of addressing both immediate resilience and long-term sustainability for farmers. The study calls for coordinated efforts among policymakers, investors, and researchers to enhance agricultural productivity and profitability in Somalia, recognizing that mere access to fertile land and favorable weather is insufficient for guaranteed success.
