Ethiopia’s growth outlook strong, but businesses face currency and execution risks - Capital Newspaper

Mon, 26 Oct 2026 17:26:11 GMT

Ethiopia is expected to achieve one of Africa's highest growth rates in 2026, with a projected real GDP growth of 9.2%, driven by agriculture, mining, and electricity generation. However, a report from the African Business Chamber (AfBC) highlights significant risks for businesses, including inflation, foreign-exchange access, and security issues. Average consumer inflation is forecasted at 11.8%, which may hinder household purchasing power and business profitability despite strong growth. The report emphasizes the need for ongoing economic reforms, particularly in foreign-exchange and monetary policies. Ethiopia's trade remains heavily reliant on a narrow export base, with coffee making up a significant portion of exports. While foreign direct investment has increased, the number of new projects has declined sharply. The AfBC advises potential investors to conduct thorough due diligence and consider phased investments to mitigate risks. Overall, Ethiopia presents long-term growth potential, but challenges related to inflation, logistics, and regulatory execution persist.
