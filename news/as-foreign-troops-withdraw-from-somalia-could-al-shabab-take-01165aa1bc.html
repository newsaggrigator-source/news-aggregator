As foreign troops withdraw from Somalia, could al-Shabab take over? - BBC

Thu, 26 Nov 2026 02:06:10 GMT

As foreign troops withdraw from Somalia, concerns are rising about the potential resurgence of the Islamist militant group al-Shabab. Analysts warn that Somali security forces may struggle to maintain stability without external support, especially amid ongoing political disputes that threaten the country's first direct elections in decades. The withdrawal of approximately 12,000 African Union troops, particularly Ugandan forces, raises fears that al-Shabab could exploit the situation to seize control of Mogadishu, as it already controls significant territory. The Somali government faces pressure to manage both the insurgency and internal political divisions, with recent attacks by al-Shabab highlighting vulnerabilities. While some officials assert that Somali forces are ready to take over security responsibilities, skepticism remains regarding their preparedness. The situation is further complicated by Somalia's heavy reliance on foreign aid, which constitutes a significant portion of its budget. As the country navigates these challenges, the risk of instability looms large.
