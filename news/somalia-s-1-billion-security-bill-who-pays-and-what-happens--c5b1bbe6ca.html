Somalia’s $1 Billion Security Bill: Who Pays, and What Happens When They Stop? - FTL Somalia

Wed, 28 Oct 2026 15:22:30 GMT

Somalia's security sector incurs over $1 billion annually, yet less than 40% of this amount is reflected in the government’s budget. The majority is funded by international partners, leading to a fragmented and unsustainable financial structure. As external support diminishes, particularly with the planned drawdown of the African Union Support and Stabilisation Mission, the Somali government faces the risk of assuming unaffordable costs or experiencing a decline in security capacity. The World Bank highlights issues such as unverified personnel on the payroll, where discrepancies indicate significant fiscal leakage. Moreover, spending is heavily skewed towards salaries rather than operational needs, resulting in a "high cost, low capability" model. The impending transition from AUSSOM raises concerns about the government's ability to manage security costs, especially as domestic revenue remains insufficient to support core functions. The 2027 draft budget reflects a decrease, exacerbating the fiscal challenges Somalia faces in maintaining security amidst dwindling international assistance.
