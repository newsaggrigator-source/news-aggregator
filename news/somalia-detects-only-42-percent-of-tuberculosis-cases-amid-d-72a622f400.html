Somalia Detects Only 42 Percent of Tuberculosis Cases Amid Deepening Crisis - Bioengineer.org

Wed, 25 Nov 2026 01:14:17 GMT

Somalia is facing a severe tuberculosis (TB) crisis, with only 42 percent of cases detected, according to the World Health Organization. The country has an estimated incidence of 246 TB cases per 100,000 people, translating to around 43,000 new cases in 2022. While treatment success for drug-sensitive TB is high, the rise of multidrug-resistant TB poses significant challenges, exacerbated by limited healthcare infrastructure and access. Many patients, especially in rural and displaced communities, struggle to receive timely diagnoses and treatment due to economic barriers and a fragmented healthcare system. The ongoing humanitarian crisis, characterized by food insecurity and displacement, further complicates TB management, as malnutrition weakens immune systems and hinders treatment adherence. International funding cuts have severely impacted TB control efforts, leading to the closure of health facilities and job losses among healthcare workers. The National TB Strategic Plan 2024–2026 aims to improve detection and integrate nutritional support into treatment regimens.
