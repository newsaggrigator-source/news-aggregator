Uganda’s exit jolts Somalia, security sovereignty faces its biggest test - The EastAfrican

Sun, 08 Nov 2026 03:00:00 GMT

Uganda's recent announcement to withdraw its troops from Somalia has raised significant concerns about the country's security sovereignty. The withdrawal follows a decision by the U.S. to halt funding for the United Nations Support Office in Somalia, which supports the African Union's peacekeeping mission. For nearly two decades, Ugandan forces have been crucial in stabilizing Somalia, particularly in regions like Lower Shabelle. The departure of these troops has left many Somali citizens anxious about the potential resurgence of Al-Shabaab, especially in areas previously secured by Ugandan forces. While Somali military leaders assert that the Somali National Army (SNA) has made progress, analysts warn that the SNA may struggle to maintain security without foreign support. Additionally, Somalia's political landscape is fractured, complicating efforts to establish a cohesive security strategy. The situation underscores a critical juncture for Somalia, as it seeks to assert its sovereignty while grappling with ongoing security challenges.
