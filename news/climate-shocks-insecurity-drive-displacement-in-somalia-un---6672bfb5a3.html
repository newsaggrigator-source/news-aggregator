Climate shocks, insecurity drive displacement in Somalia: UN - Xinhua

Tue, 17 Nov 2026 22:23:15 GMT

Severe climate shocks and ongoing insecurity are driving significant displacement in Somalia, according to the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA). Currently, over 3.3 million people, primarily women, children, and the elderly, are displaced across the country. From January to September 2026, more than 800,000 individuals were newly displaced, with 70% attributed to drought conditions. The situation is expected to worsen with projected El Niño-related flooding, which could displace hundreds of thousands more by December. Recent flooding has already affected over 170,000 people. Additionally, violence in the Bay and Bari regions has led to further displacements. The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) highlighted that armed conflict and food insecurity are exacerbating the humanitarian crisis, with funding shortfalls leading to reduced assistance and the closure of health facilities. The Somalia humanitarian response plan is severely underfunded, receiving only 36% of the required $852 million.
