Surveillance Gaps Mask Thirtyfold Malaria Burden Across Rainy Seasons in Somalia - Bioengineer.org

Sun, 22 Nov 2026 23:28:35 GMT

A significant surveillance gap in Somalia has resulted in a stark underreporting of malaria cases, with only 32,983 confirmed cases documented in 2024, compared to an estimated 1,012,760 infections by the World Health Organization. Researchers highlight that seasonal rainfall exacerbates malaria transmission, particularly in riverine areas, yet systemic healthcare deficiencies obscure the true scale of the epidemic. Vulnerable populations, especially children under five and pregnant women, are disproportionately affected. Displacement due to flooding has worsened the situation, creating breeding grounds for mosquitoes and limiting access to healthcare. Many individuals bypass public health facilities, seeking care in the unregulated private sector, which further complicates surveillance efforts. The closure of health centers due to funding shortages and the prevalence of malnutrition among children compound the crisis. Experts advocate for community-centered interventions, improved surveillance, and sustainable domestic resource mobilization to address these challenges and enhance malaria control efforts in Somalia.
