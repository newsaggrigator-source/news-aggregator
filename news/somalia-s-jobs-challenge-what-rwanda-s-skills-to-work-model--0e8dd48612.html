Somalia’s jobs challenge: What Rwanda’s skills-to-work model could teach - Dawan Africa

Mon, 26 Oct 2026 07:55:24 GMT

Somalia faces a significant jobs challenge, with a pressing need for economic reforms to create employment opportunities for its youth. The World Bank estimates that proposed public finance reforms could generate approximately 54,000 new jobs and improve 250,000 existing positions by 2033, but these figures depend on sustained implementation. Currently, Somalia requires about 100,000 jobs annually just to maintain existing employment levels. The experience of Rwanda offers valuable insights, where a skills-to-work program trained nearly 24,000 individuals, resulting in 82% finding employment shortly after. This model emphasizes collaboration between employers and training institutions to align skills with market demands. As Somalia seeks to enhance its public finances, it must focus not only on job creation but also on ensuring that these jobs lead to sustainable livelihoods. The challenge lies in fostering a labor market where young people can transition from training to meaningful employment, ultimately contributing to economic growth and poverty reduction.
