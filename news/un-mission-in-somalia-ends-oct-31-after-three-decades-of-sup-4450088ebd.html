UN mission in Somalia ends Oct. 31 after three decades of support - Yeni Şafak English

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 07:14:14 GMT

The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) will officially conclude its operations on October 31, 2026, marking the end of over three decades of UN peacekeeping efforts in the country. This decision follows a formal request from Somalia's federal government and aligns with Security Council resolutions. UN spokesperson Stephane Dujarric highlighted that the mission's closure signifies a transition roadmap agreed upon by UN officials and Somali authorities, allowing for a gradual transfer of responsibilities to Somali institutions. Despite the mission's end, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres emphasized the UN's ongoing commitment to Somalia through resident agencies and development programs, aiming to support peace, stability, and human rights. He called for sustained international support to address the significant challenges that remain in Somalia, including political dialogue, reconciliation, and institutional strengthening. UNTMIS was established in May 2021 to facilitate Somalia's security transition following the phased withdrawal of the African Union Transition Mission.
