Ethiopia and the Red Sea: A Civilizational Case for Maritime Access. Perspectives from the 12th Tana High-Level Forum on Security in Africa - IFA - Institute of Foreign Affairs

Mon, 02 Nov 2026 07:29:04 GMT

Ethiopia, the world's most populous landlocked country, emphasizes the critical need for maritime access to the Red Sea, as articulated by Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed at the 12th Tana High-Level Forum. He argues that access to the sea is essential for Ethiopia's economic sovereignty and security, especially given its historical maritime connections dating back to the Aksumite Empire. The forum highlighted Ethiopia's unique position among large historical states that maintain coastal access, underscoring the strategic disadvantages it faces since losing direct access to the Red Sea in 1993. Abiy's government seeks to address this gap through dialogue and mutual benefit, advocating for a collaborative approach to Red Sea security that includes both coastal and non-coastal states. The Tana Forum serves as a platform for Ethiopia to articulate its maritime aspirations while promoting regional stability and cooperation. The government aims to strengthen existing trade routes and engage in diplomatic efforts to secure reliable access to the sea. (160 words)
