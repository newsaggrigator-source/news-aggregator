Ethiopia in the Face of Catastrophe: Why Is Civil Resistance Not Rising to the Scale of National Suffering? - Borkena

Sun, 15 Nov 2026 00:28:36 GMT

Ethiopia is currently facing a severe national crisis marked by widespread poverty, armed conflicts, mass displacement, and political repression. Millions are suffering from loss, insecurity, and despair, yet civil resistance has not emerged to the extent that the situation demands. The article explores the paradox of this apparent political passivity, particularly among the youth, despite the dire circumstances. Factors contributing to this lack of collective action include fear of retaliation, political disillusionment, and the fragmentation of social identities. The author reflects on personal experiences from a previous generation, contrasting the intense political engagement of youth then with today's apparent apathy. The pervasive climate of fear, exacerbated by authoritarian rule and repression, stifles dissent and discourages organized resistance. Ultimately, the article emphasizes the need for understanding the structural conditions that inhibit collective mobilization and the importance of reclaiming agency and dignity to envision a more just political future for Ethiopia.
