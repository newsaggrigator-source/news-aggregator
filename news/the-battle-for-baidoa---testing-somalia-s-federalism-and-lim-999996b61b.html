The Battle for Baidoa - Testing Somalia's Federalism and Limits of Presidential Power

Tue, 03 Nov 2026 15:22:17 GMT

The political landscape in Somalia, particularly in Baidoa, is under intense scrutiny as it highlights the challenges of federalism and presidential authority. Baidoa serves as a crucial political hub where clan representation and regional autonomy intersect. The recent conflict arose from a power struggle between President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud and Abdiaziz Hassan Mohamed Laftagareen, leading to Laftagareen's resignation and the controversial election of Sheikh Aden Mohamed as his successor. This shift, backed by the federal government, has not resolved underlying grievances, as Laftagareen's supporters continue to capture strategic towns. The situation underscores the fragility of Somalia's federal system, where military control does not equate to political legitimacy. Furthermore, constitutional amendments extending presidential terms have sparked dissent, raising questions about the legitimacy of governance. The crisis reflects broader tensions between federal authority and regional autonomy, with implications for national stability and the future of Somalia's political framework. The need for transparent governance and respect for regional autonomy remains critical. (159 words)
