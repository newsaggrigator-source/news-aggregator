Uganda signals Somalia military exit as rifts hurt war on Shabaab - The EastAfrican

Fri, 30 Oct 2026 18:30:00 GMT

Uganda has announced the withdrawal of its additional troops from Somalia, raising concerns about the country's ability to maintain military gains against Al Shabaab amid deepening political divisions. Uganda's Chief of Defence Forces, Muhoozi Kainerugaba, confirmed the troop withdrawal shortly after discussions between President Yoweri Museveni and Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud regarding Uganda's military presence. The withdrawal comes at a critical time when Somalia's political landscape is fragmented, complicating efforts to establish a unified front against the Islamist insurgency. Political disputes, particularly in South West State, have exacerbated tensions between the federal government and regional administrations, undermining security efforts. While Uganda has played a significant role in supporting Somali forces since 2007, the sustainability of military gains now hinges on Somalia's ability to foster political cohesion. The potential exit of Ugandan troops could further expose vulnerabilities, allowing Al Shabaab to exploit existing divisions within the Somali state.
