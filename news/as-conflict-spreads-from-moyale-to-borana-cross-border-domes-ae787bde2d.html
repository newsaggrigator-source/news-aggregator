As Conflict Spreads From Moyale to Borana, Cross Border, Domestic Livestock Markets Grind to a Halt

Thu, 12 Nov 2026 05:23:12 GMT

The ongoing conflict in Ethiopia, which began in Moyale and has spread to Borana, is severely disrupting livestock markets, a vital part of the local economy. Residents report that the fighting has led to a near-total halt in livestock trade, with prices plummeting as traders from Ethiopia and neighboring Kenya withdraw from the region. For instance, the price of a young bull has dropped from 120,000-150,000 Birr to just 70,000-90,000 Birr. The absence of traders has left local pastoralists vulnerable, forcing them to sell livestock at significantly reduced prices to meet immediate needs. An economist noted that market activity has contracted by over 90%, affecting not only pastoralists but also small traders and local businesses. The situation poses a risk of long-term economic damage, with potential hunger and malnutrition threatening the community. Urgent interventions are needed to stabilize the market and support affected households.
