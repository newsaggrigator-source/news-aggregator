Somalia and the ‘Bilateralisation’ of the War Against Al-Shabaab and ISIS - Realnews Magazine

Fri, 30 Oct 2026 11:21:04 GMT

Somalia's fight against Al-Shabaab and ISIS is increasingly defined by "bilateralisation," characterized by Somali-led operations supported by international military forces. This approach has tactical advantages but raises concerns about coordination and sustainability, particularly regarding the future role of the African Union Support and Stabilization Mission in Somalia (AUSSOM). Recent operations in Lower Shabelle, backed by Ugandan troops and U.S. airstrikes, highlight the effectiveness of this model. The U.S. has conducted 88 airstrikes in 2026, while Turkey has expanded its military presence through training and defense cooperation. Bilateral troop deployments from Uganda and Ethiopia further complicate the operational landscape. While these arrangements can enhance military effectiveness, they also risk creating dependency on foreign support. The challenge lies in ensuring that these tactical gains translate into lasting security and that Somali forces can independently maintain stability. The future of AUSSOM and the integration of bilateral efforts will be crucial in shaping Somalia's security strategy. (159 words)
