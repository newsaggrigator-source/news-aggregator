Entrenched Authoritarianism: Ethiopia's Rule of Law Index Slides Further

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 05:14:49 GMT

Ethiopia's rule of law has significantly declined, ranking 133rd out of 143 countries in the 2026 World Justice Project (WJP) Rule of Law Index. This marks a continuation of a multi-year downward trend, with Ethiopia consistently near the bottom in both global and sub-Saharan African comparisons. The WJP's report highlights a global recession in rule of law, with 62% of assessed countries experiencing declines. Ethiopia's specific weaknesses include constraints on government power, fundamental rights, and open government, with rankings of 134th, 138th, and 135th respectively. The country has faced ongoing armed conflicts and serious human rights violations, including extrajudicial killings and restrictions on freedom of expression, which exacerbate its institutional challenges. The media environment remains under pressure, with numerous journalists reported detained or harassed. Overall, Ethiopia's persistent low rankings reflect deep-rooted issues in governance, accountability, and civic freedoms, underscoring the need for significant reforms to improve its rule of law.
