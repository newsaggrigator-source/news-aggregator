Power relations and agency in Somalia’s election process - Hiiraan Online

Sun, 15 Nov 2026 15:20:18 GMT

Somalia's electoral process is characterized by a complex interplay of power relations and agency, shaped by its historical context. Since gaining independence in 1960, Somalia has experienced significant political shifts, transitioning from democratic elections to an authoritarian regime following the 1969 coup. The international community advocates for universal suffrage, while Somali political actors often favor clan-based electoral systems, reflecting local realities and historical experiences. This divergence raises questions about political authority and legitimacy within Somalia's state-building efforts. Clan structures, rooted in pre-colonial governance, continue to play a crucial role in maintaining social order and facilitating political negotiations. The international community's push for universal suffrage is seen as a means to enhance accountability and reduce clan influence, yet it risks overlooking local contexts. Ultimately, the Somali electoral process highlights the ongoing negotiation of power and agency, revealing tensions between international norms and locally defined priorities in shaping the country's political future.
