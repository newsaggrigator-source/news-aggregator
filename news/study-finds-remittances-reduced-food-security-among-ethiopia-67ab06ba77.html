Study Finds Remittances Reduced Food Security Among Ethiopian Agro-Pastoralists - Bioengineer.org

Tue, 17 Nov 2026 19:00:26 GMT

A recent study conducted in Lare District, Nuer Zone, Gambella Region, Ethiopia, revealed that remittances negatively impact food security among agro-pastoralist households. The research found that households receiving remittances experienced a decrease in food security by 0.4267 units compared to those that did not receive any remittance support. Utilizing a mixed-methods approach, the study surveyed 384 respondents and conducted key informant interviews to gather data. Results indicated that 46.6% of respondents received remittances, while 53.39% did not. The study identified various factors influencing food security among remittance users, including livestock holding size, cultivated land size, and distance to water. In contrast, the educational level of the respondent was significant for non-remittance users. The findings underscore the need for targeted policies that enhance productive investments in remittances and promote sustainable food security strategies for agro-pastoralist communities in the region.
