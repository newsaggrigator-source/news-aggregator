The Sodere Initiative and Ethiopia’s Role in Somali State-Building - horn review

Wed, 21 Oct 2026 15:11:09 GMT

The Sodere Initiative, launched by Ethiopia in late 1996, aimed to unify Somalia's rival factions during a period of political fragmentation following the collapse of the Somali state in 1991. Convened under the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD), the talks led to the establishment of a 41-member National Salvation Council, representing major clan families and smaller communities. While the initiative did not create a lasting government, it laid the groundwork for future reconciliation efforts by promoting clan-family parity in political representation. Ethiopia's involvement was driven by security concerns, particularly regarding the Islamist group Al-Itihaad al-Islamiya, which posed threats to its border. The initiative's legacy continues to influence Somalia's ongoing security challenges, particularly in light of Al-Shabaab's presence and the need for coordinated regional security efforts as Somalia transitions towards greater self-governance.
