Breaking Ethiopia’s Non-Cooperation Trap - Borkena

Sun, 15 Nov 2026 20:50:53 GMT

Ethiopia's ongoing political crisis is characterized by a self-reinforcing non-cooperative equilibrium, where various actors—government leaders, opposition parties, and civil society—prefer peace but remain entrenched in conflict. This situation is analyzed through the lens of Nash equilibrium, illustrating how mutual mistrust leads to a costly standoff rather than cooperation. The article emphasizes that Ethiopia's political culture is marred by historical grievances, fear of domination, and a lack of credible commitments, which perpetuates the cycle of violence and instability. To break this cycle, the author proposes a four-phase roadmap focused on cooperation, accountability, and rebuilding trust among stakeholders. Key steps include protecting civilians, negotiating guarantees, reconstructing services, and institutionalizing accountable governance. The article argues that without a shift from a zero-sum mentality to a cooperative framework, Ethiopia's path to peace and stability will remain obstructed, necessitating a fundamental change in the strategic environment that influences political choices.
