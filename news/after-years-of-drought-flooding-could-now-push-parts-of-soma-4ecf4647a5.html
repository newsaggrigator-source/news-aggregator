After years of drought, flooding could now push parts of Somalia toward famine - Horseed Media

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 15:58:06 GMT

After years of drought, Somalia faces a new threat as forecasts predict severe flooding due to the upcoming El Niño season, potentially pushing vulnerable communities toward famine. Following a recent drought that narrowly avoided famine, many health centers and food assistance programs have closed due to funding shortages. The anticipated Deyr rains, typically occurring from October to December, could lead to flash floods, displacing millions. In 2023, flooding displaced 1.2 million people, and this year, estimates suggest 2.5 to 3.5 million could be affected, with up to 1 million at risk of further displacement. The situation is exacerbated by the closure of over 230 nutrition centers, leaving 1.1 million women and children without essential services. Disease outbreaks, including cholera and measles, further threaten malnourished populations. Humanitarian organizations are calling for increased funding and support to prepare for the impending floods, emphasizing the need for timely action to prevent a humanitarian disaster.
