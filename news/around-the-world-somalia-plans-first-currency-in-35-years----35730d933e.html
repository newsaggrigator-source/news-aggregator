Around the World: Somalia Plans First Currency in 35 Years - Numismatic News

Fri, 23 Oct 2026 07:04:00 GMT

Somalia is planning to introduce its first new currency in 35 years, as the country continues to struggle with outdated and deteriorating banknotes. The Somali economy has been heavily dollarized due to decades of conflict, including a civil war from 1991 to 2006, which left the nation one of the least developed globally. Currently, the Somali shilling is largely untrusted, with a 2017 IMF report indicating that 98% of circulating banknotes were counterfeit. The transitional government has attempted to revive the Central Bank of Somalia, but inflation and a lack of confidence have hindered progress. Meanwhile, Somaliland, which declared independence in 1994, issues its own currency, complicating the situation further. The Somali government is under pressure to establish a viable national currency, with Commerce Minister Gamal Mohamed Hassan stating that issuing new banknotes is a priority. However, no confirmed plans or timelines for the new currency have been announced, leaving the future of Somalia's monetary policy uncertain.
