The Micro-Enterprise Trap: Why 98% of Somali Firms Are Untaxable - FTL Somalia

Sat, 07 Nov 2026 12:07:00 GMT

A recent World Bank report highlights the challenges facing Somalia's economy, where 98% of firms are micro-enterprises, making them nearly untaxable. The report reveals that out of 174,149 establishments, 47% are micro-sized, employing between one and nine workers. This overwhelming presence of micro-enterprises limits the government's ability to generate revenue through traditional taxation methods, as these businesses often operate informally and lack proper records. The 2025 Income Tax Law attempted to simplify taxation for small businesses, but concerns remain about its regressivity. Additionally, Al-Shabaab imposes its own taxation system, further complicating the government's efforts to expand its tax base. While many Somalis express a willingness to pay taxes, trust in the government to use funds effectively is low. The report concludes that without addressing the dual challenges of Al-Shabaab's parallel taxation and the lack of a social contract, Somalia's revenue base will remain fragile and narrow.
