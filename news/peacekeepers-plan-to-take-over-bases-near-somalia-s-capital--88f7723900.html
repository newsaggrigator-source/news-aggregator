Peacekeepers plan to take over bases near Somalia’s capital as Ugandan troops withdraw - The Spec

Tue, 03 Nov 2026 09:12:58 GMT

Peacekeepers are set to take over four military bases near Mogadishu, Somalia, as Ugandan troops begin their withdrawal after a 20-year deployment. This transition aims to prevent al-Shabab militants from exploiting the security vacuum left by the departing Ugandan forces. Uganda's withdrawal is part of a broader plan, with all troops expected to leave by the end of 2027. The African Union Support and Stabilization Mission in Somalia will assume control of the bases—Sabid-Anole, Bariire, Awdheegle, and Bulonagad—during troop rotations scheduled for November. The Ugandan troops had previously played a crucial role in recapturing territory from al-Shabab. However, concerns remain regarding the Somali army's capability to secure these bases, given its limited resources and ongoing militant threats. The withdrawal coincides with the closure of the United Nations' political mission in Somalia, marking a significant shift in the country's security and governance landscape.
