Toyo Suspends Imports of Ethiopian-Made Solar Cells to U.S. Amid Ongoing Trade Probe

Wed, 18 Nov 2026 05:32:25 GMT

Japanese solar manufacturer TOYO Co., Ltd. has halted imports of solar cells produced in Ethiopia to the U.S. due to an ongoing investigation by the U.S. Department of Commerce. The inquiry focuses on allegations of tariff circumvention involving Chinese components. During a recent earnings call, TOYO's Chief Strategy Officer, Rhone Resch, confirmed the suspension, emphasizing the uncertainty surrounding the investigation. Although U.S. Customs and Border Protection had released previously detained shipments after reviewing supply-chain documentation, the separate investigation remains unresolved. TOYO asserts that it does not use Chinese-origin materials in its Ethiopian production, sourcing wafers from Southeast Asia and polysilicon from the U.S. and Malaysia. To fulfill U.S. customer commitments, the company has secured alternative solar cell supplies. Despite the challenges, TOYO continues to expand its manufacturing operations in the U.S., including a new facility in Texas, while reporting a revenue decline in the third quarter of 2026. A preliminary determination from the investigation is expected by mid-December.
