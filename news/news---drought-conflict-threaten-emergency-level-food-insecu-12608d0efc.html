News - Drought, Conflict Threaten Emergency-Level Food Insecurity in Tigray, Amhara, Afar

Thu, 12 Nov 2026 15:23:31 GMT

Severe drought and escalating conflict are projected to push parts of Ethiopia's Tigray, Amhara, and Afar regions into Emergency-level food insecurity (IPC Phase 4) by early 2027, according to the Famine Early Warning Systems Network (FEWS NET). The report highlights widespread crop losses, disrupted markets, and declining household purchasing power as key factors exacerbating the humanitarian crisis. The June-September kiremt rainy season has seen historically low rainfall, leading to significant deficits in crop yields. Areas particularly at risk include eastern and southern Tigray, parts of Amhara, and northern Afar, where near-total crop failures are anticipated. The ongoing conflict has further hindered agricultural production and market access, with many households resorting to desperate measures for food. Humanitarian assistance has also declined sharply, with a 73% drop in food aid from August to September 2026. While some regions may see seasonal improvements, the overall outlook remains dire, with many households facing acute food shortages and malnutrition risks.
