Somalia Faces Rising Rift Valley Fever Risk, Threatening Livestock Livelihoods - Hiiraan Online

Sat, 31 Oct 2026 19:00:34 GMT

Somalia is facing an increased risk of Rift Valley fever, which threatens the livelihoods of communities dependent on livestock. The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the regional bloc IGAD have identified Somalia among six countries at high risk due to favorable conditions for the disease's spread, exacerbated by El Niño-related rainfall. Although no outbreak has been confirmed, the FAO's assessment warns of newly suitable transmission areas in southern Somalia and predicts that these conditions may persist through January 2027. The disease poses significant economic threats, as it can lead to severe illness and death in livestock, impacting rural households reliant on these animals. The FAO and IGAD have urged for enhanced surveillance, vaccination preparedness, and cross-border coordination to mitigate the risks. Historical precedents, such as the major outbreak in 1997-98, highlight the importance of proactive measures to safeguard both animal health and human safety in the region.
