Somalia and Somaliland’s Spiral of Mutual Destruction - Geeska

Wed, 11 Nov 2026 08:27:54 GMT

The relationship between Somalia and Somaliland, once a voluntary union formed in 1960, has devolved into a destructive rivalry, drawing in external powers like Ethiopia, Türkiye, Israel, and Saudi Arabia. Initially united under a pan-Somali project, the partnership soured due to historical grievances, particularly following the brutal regime of Major General Mohamed Siad Barre, which led to Somaliland's declaration of independence in 1991. Since then, Somaliland has established a stable political system, while Somalia has struggled with governance and security, particularly against the al-Shabaab insurgency. Both regions have sought foreign alliances, often at the expense of their sovereignty, leading to economic tensions, such as disputes over livestock exports. The situation has escalated with Somaliland's recognition by Israel and Somalia's military agreements with Türkiye and Saudi Arabia. The article argues for a framework of peaceful coexistence, suggesting that cooperation could mitigate the ongoing costs of their rivalry and enhance regional stability.
