Winning Wars, Losing Peace - Ethiopia's Unfinished Political Struggle for Lasting Stability

Fri, 06 Nov 2026 04:11:50 GMT

Ethiopia, once hopeful for peace under Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed in 2018, now grapples with escalating armed conflicts and political instability. As of October 2026, renewed violence in Tigray, ongoing clashes in Amhara, and insurgencies in Oromia highlight the country's precarious situation. Tensions with Eritrea and localized violence further exacerbate the crisis, threatening millions' lives and livelihoods. The government's reliance on military solutions risks deepening divisions and undermining public trust, as prolonged conflict disrupts education and economic stability. Ethnic federalism and unresolved territorial disputes create conditions ripe for future conflict. The article emphasizes that true victory lies not in military dominance but in establishing a sustainable political order through dialogue and compromise. Ethiopia's leadership faces a critical choice: to pursue peace through negotiation or continue a cycle of violence. Without a commitment to addressing grievances and fostering trust, the end of one conflict may only lead to the next.
