Consistency in Ethiopia’s Approach and the Evolution of Mediation Channels from IGAD - horn review

Wed, 21 Oct 2026 15:58:26 GMT

The article discusses the evolution of Ethiopia's mediation strategies in relation to its historical conflicts with Somalia, particularly since the mid-1980s. It highlights the consistency of Ethiopia's core security interests while adapting to changing diplomatic contexts, illustrated by the 1988 agreement and the 2024 Ankara Declaration. The 1988 agreement, facilitated by IGAD, marked a significant step in stabilizing relations by addressing border disputes and ending mutual support for insurgent groups. In contrast, the 2024 Ankara Declaration, brokered by Turkey, aimed to de-escalate tensions following Ethiopia's agreement with Somaliland, which Somalia viewed as a threat to its sovereignty. The article emphasizes that successful mediation relies on aligning core interests and establishing credible dialogue channels, regardless of the mediator's identity. Overall, Ethiopia's approach has remained focused on safeguarding its territorial integrity and regional stability.
