Contested Land, Historical Grievances - Tracing Borana Pastoralists' Territorial Loss and Roots of Moyale Conflict

Tue, 03 Nov 2026 06:34:12 GMT

The historical grievances and territorial losses of the Borana pastoralists in Ethiopia have intensified the ongoing conflict in Moyale. Research indicates that the Borana territory once extended into parts of Kenya and Somalia, but over the last 150 years, they have faced significant encroachment from Somali groups, particularly during the Ethiopia-Somalia war of 1977-78. This conflict led to the loss of crucial grazing areas and water resources, undermining the pastoral livelihoods of the Borana. The situation worsened after the collapse of the Somali state in 1991, resulting in an influx of returnees who settled on Borana land, further complicating territorial claims. Recent tensions have escalated due to disputes over administrative boundaries and the raising of the Somali Regional State flag in Moyale. The conflict has resulted in violence, civilian displacement, and a humanitarian crisis, necessitating urgent dialogue and intervention to address the underlying issues and protect affected communities.
