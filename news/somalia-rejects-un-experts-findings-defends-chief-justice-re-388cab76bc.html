Somalia Rejects UN Experts’ Findings, Defends Chief Justice Removal as Transitional Measure - FTL Somalia

Sat, 14 Nov 2026 09:07:51 GMT

The Somali government has rejected the findings of UN human rights experts who deemed the dismissal of Chief Justice Bashe Yusuf Ahmed unlawful. The Ministry of Justice and Constitutional Affairs defended the November 4 removal, asserting it was executed within a transitional legal framework as Somalia shifts to the 2026 Constitution. The government claims that key institutions, such as the High Council of the Judiciary, are not yet established, and thus the removal adhered to the same transitional process used for his appointment. The UN experts criticized the dismissal as interference with judicial independence and called for the Chief Justice's reinstatement. However, the Somali government contends that the former Chief Justice's pronouncements were invalid as he had already been removed from office. While the government remains open to dialogue with the UN, the ongoing constitutional crisis persists, with judges lacking protection and the Supreme Court's rulings unaddressed. The situation highlights a significant legal and procedural disagreement between Somalia and the UN.
