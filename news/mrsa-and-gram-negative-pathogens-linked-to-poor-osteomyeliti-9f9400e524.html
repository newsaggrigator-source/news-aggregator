MRSA and Gram-Negative Pathogens Linked to Poor Osteomyelitis Outcomes in Somalia - Bioengineer.org

Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:18:42 GMT

A retrospective cohort study conducted at a tertiary care hospital in Mogadishu, Somalia, has found a significant association between methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and Gram-negative pathogens with poor outcomes in osteomyelitis patients. Analyzing clinical records from January 2022 to July 2026, the study included 490 patients, predominantly young males, with chronic osteomyelitis being the most common presentation. The tibia was the most affected site, and 63.9% of infections were linked to physical trauma. Treatment varied, with 68.6% undergoing surgical intervention. Of the 448 patients with recorded outcomes, 44.6% experienced treatment failure. Notably, Gram-negative bacteria were linked to a fourfold increase in the risk of poor outcomes. The study emphasizes the need for further research, including prospective studies with comprehensive antibiotic tracking, to validate these findings and improve treatment strategies for osteomyelitis in Somalia.
