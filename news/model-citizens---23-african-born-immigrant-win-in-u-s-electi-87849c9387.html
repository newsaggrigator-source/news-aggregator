Model Citizens - 23 African-Born Immigrant Win in U.S. Elections

Mon, 09 Nov 2026 20:40:37 GMT

In a historic achievement, twenty-three African-born immigrants from at least ten African countries won elections in the United States, marking a significant increase in political visibility for this demographic. Among the victors, fourteen are Nigerian-Americans, celebrated back home for their accomplishments. This success is seen as a rebuke to stereotypes associating blackness with underachievement, as many African immigrants excel in various fields, including education and technology. However, the triumphs are juxtaposed with ongoing challenges, such as racism and harassment faced by elected officials like Deqa Dhalac, the first African-born female mayor in the U.S. The article highlights the contrast between the modern conception of citizenship in the U.S. and the static notions prevalent in many African nations, emphasizing the need for African leaders to address the systemic issues driving talent away from the continent. Ultimately, the article calls for a commitment to making Africa a viable home for its citizens. (158 words)
