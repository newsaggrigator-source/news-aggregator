The United Nations has officially closed its political mission in Somalia, marking the end of over 30 years of engagement aimed at aiding the country's recovery from civil war. The United Nations Transitional Assistance Mission in Somalia (UNTMIS) ceased operations after a two-year transition requested by the Somali government. This transition involved transferring responsibilities to Somali institutions and UN agencies. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres acknowledged Somalia's progress but emphasized the need for ongoing international support amid political and humanitarian challenges. The closure follows a formal request from President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's government in 2024 and reflects strained relations between the UN and Somali authorities. While the UN's humanitarian and development agencies remain, they face funding cuts, impacting essential services. The departure of the UN mission raises concerns about political stability, as Somalia continues to grapple with internal divisions and threats from the militant group al-Shabab.
