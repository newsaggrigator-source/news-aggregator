The Corridor We Must Force Open - a Tigrayan Woman's Reckoning

Thu, 29 Oct 2026 15:33:21 GMT

The article discusses the profound challenges faced by Tigray during and after the conflict, highlighting the failures of governance and the resilience of community solidarity. While Tigrayans relied on long-established social networks for survival amidst the crisis, the lack of effective institutions and accountable leadership exacerbated their vulnerabilities. The author emphasizes the need for honest assessments of the crisis and the importance of rebuilding civic values, public services, and inclusive governance. The peace process, particularly the Pretoria agreement, lacked meaningful representation from affected communities, leading to a disconnect between political decisions and public needs. The article argues for the inclusion of women and marginalized groups in leadership roles to foster collaboration and accountability. It warns against the myth of the "Alpha Male" leader, advocating instead for a political culture that values collective decision-making and civic engagement. Ultimately, the author calls for a societal shift towards building resilient institutions that prioritize the common good over individual power struggles. (158 words)
