The Red Sea Chokehold: Why Maritime Access Is an Existential Question for Ethiopia - horn review

Sat, 31 Oct 2026 06:56:15 GMT

Ethiopia's quest for maritime access to the Red Sea is a critical issue that transcends mere economic considerations, becoming an existential matter for the nation. As the most populous landlocked country, Ethiopia's reliance on Djibouti for 95% of its trade exposes it to significant vulnerabilities, particularly given the militarization of the Red Sea and the geopolitical tensions in the region. The recent formation of alliances among Egypt, Eritrea, and Somalia aims to isolate Ethiopia, further complicating its access to vital maritime routes. This situation is exacerbated by external military presences that threaten Ethiopia's sovereignty and economic stability. Ethiopia's demand for a reliable maritime corridor is not merely an expansionist ambition but a legitimate right essential for its survival and security. The international community, alongside regional organizations, must facilitate dialogue to address Ethiopia's concerns while ensuring the interests of neighboring states are respected, to prevent potential conflict and promote regional stability.
