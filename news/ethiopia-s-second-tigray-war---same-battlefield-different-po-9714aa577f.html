Ethiopia's Second Tigray War - Same Battlefield, Different Political Reality

Mon, 16 Nov 2026 15:11:36 GMT

Ethiopia's Second Tigray War has unfolded against a backdrop of shifting political dynamics since the initial conflict in 2020. After federal forces initially captured Mekelle, Tigrayan fighters regained control within months, leading to a renewed offensive. By October 2026, federal troops, supported by Tigrayan allies, retook Mekelle, but the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) vowed to continue fighting. The political landscape has drastically changed, with former adversaries now forming alliances, complicating the conflict. The Ethiopian government’s focus on territorial control does not address deeper issues of sovereignty and political legitimacy. The current war is intertwined with regional rivalries, particularly with Eritrea, which has escalated tensions. While federal forces maintain military advantages, the challenges of sustaining control over captured territories and managing multiple fronts are significant. The ongoing conflict risks further destabilization, as historical grievances and ethnic divisions continue to shape Ethiopia's political landscape. The need for a comprehensive dialogue on governance and accountability remains critical for lasting peace.
