Six Somali Supreme Court judges sue federal government at East African Court of Justice - Hiiraan Online

Mon, 23 Nov 2026 08:25:40 GMT

Six judges from the Somali Supreme Court, including Chief Justice Baashe Yusuf Ahmed, have initiated legal action against the federal government at the East African Court of Justice (EACJ). The lawsuit, filed on November 18, challenges the government's recent actions following President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's dismissal of Baashe. The judges are contesting the alleged interference with the Supreme Court's independence and operations, particularly after a ruling on constitutional amendments extending presidential and parliamentary terms. The EACJ has given the Somali attorney general 45 days to respond, warning that failure to do so may lead to a ruling in the government's absence. The controversy has drawn international criticism, with UN experts condemning Baashe's dismissal as an infringement on judicial independence. This case adds a new layer to Somalia's ongoing political crisis, marked by disputes over constitutional reforms and the timeline for national elections. The federal government has yet to announce its response to the EACJ proceedings.
