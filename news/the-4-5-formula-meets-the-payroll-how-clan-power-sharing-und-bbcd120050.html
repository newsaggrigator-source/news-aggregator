The 4.5 Formula Meets the Payroll: How Clan Power-Sharing Undermines Meritocracy - FTL Somalia

Fri, 30 Oct 2026 10:05:03 GMT

The article discusses the challenges facing Somalia's civil service, particularly how the 4.5 clan power-sharing formula undermines meritocracy. Despite a small public administration, the civilian wage bill consumes a significant portion of the government's budget, limiting funds for essential services. The civil service is fragmented, with only a fraction of employees formally recognized, leading to issues with accountability and transparency. Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre's attempts to implement merit-based recruitment were thwarted by parliamentary resistance, which cited constitutional discrepancies. This resistance highlights the tension between political interests and the need for effective governance. The 4.5 formula, while intended to ensure representation, has resulted in nepotism and patronage, compromising the quality of public service. The article emphasizes the need for political commitment to reform, as current practices prioritize clan affiliation over qualifications, hindering the establishment of a competent and efficient civil service capable of meeting citizens' needs.
