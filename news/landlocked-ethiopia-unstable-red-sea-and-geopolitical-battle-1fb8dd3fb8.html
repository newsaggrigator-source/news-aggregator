Landlocked Ethiopia, Unstable Red Sea, and Geopolitical battle in the Horn of Africa - Borkena

Mon, 26 Oct 2026 13:52:27 GMT

Ethiopia is grappling with multiple insurgencies, economic distress, and a legitimacy crisis, threatening its national survival. The country, landlocked and reliant on a single port, seeks maritime access to enhance regional integration and security. This quest has heightened tensions with neighbors, particularly Egypt, which has formed alliances with Eritrea and Somalia to counter Ethiopia's ambitions. The geopolitical landscape is further complicated by rivalries among Gulf states, with the UAE supporting Ethiopia while Saudi Arabia aligns with Egypt and its allies. Amidst this turmoil, seven opposition groups have united to form the Ethiopian People's Forces Alliance for Survival, aiming to overthrow the government. The ongoing conflict is not merely a civil war but a struggle over Ethiopia's identity and governance. A political solution is essential for stability, requiring internal dialogue and external pressure from the international community to facilitate negotiations and reforms. Without genuine political engagement, Ethiopia risks further fragmentation and regional insecurity.
