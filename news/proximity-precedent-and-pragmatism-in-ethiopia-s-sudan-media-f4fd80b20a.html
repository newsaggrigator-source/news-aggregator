Proximity, Precedent, and Pragmatism in Ethiopia’s Sudan Mediation Bid - horn review

Wed, 25 Nov 2026 14:57:15 GMT

Ethiopia's Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has initiated a significant diplomatic effort to mediate the ongoing conflict in Sudan, which has resulted in over 200,000 deaths and severe humanitarian crises. On November 24, 2026, he invited Sudanese military, political, and civilian actors to engage in an inclusive dialogue in Addis Ababa. Ethiopia's geographical proximity and historical involvement in Sudanese affairs lend credibility to its mediation proposal, which is structured around four key pillars: facilitating a ceasefire, establishing a national unity government, ensuring Sudanese ownership of the process, and leveraging diplomatic networks for external support. Despite the Sudanese Armed Forces' rejection of the initiative, citing concerns over Ethiopia's neutrality, major Sudanese factions have expressed willingness to engage. The success of this mediation will depend on the inclusion of diverse stakeholders, particularly civil society groups, and the appointment of a dedicated Ethiopian envoy to sustain diplomatic efforts. Ethiopia's longstanding commitment to regional stability positions it uniquely to address Sudan's crisis.
