Open for Business, Closed for Justice: The Reality of Investing in Somalia - FTL Somalia

Fri, 30 Oct 2026 10:30:21 GMT

The Federal Government of Somalia has implemented various laws to attract foreign investment, including the 2025 Investment Law, which established the Somalia Investment Promotion Agency. However, the U.S. Department of State's 2026 Investment Climate Statement reveals significant challenges that hinder investment. Despite an expanded legal framework, the lack of a functioning judicial system, rampant corruption, and a financial sector isolated from global banking severely deter potential investors. Somalia's courts are viewed as corrupt, and many investors resort to clan mediation or international arbitration. Additionally, the financial sector's constraints lead to high intermediary fees, costing the economy around $210 million annually. Al-Shabaab's control over large areas and its extortion of businesses further complicate the investment landscape. While the government has made strides in reforming investment laws, the absence of effective institutions and ongoing security threats create an environment where attracting and sustaining investment remains a daunting challenge.
