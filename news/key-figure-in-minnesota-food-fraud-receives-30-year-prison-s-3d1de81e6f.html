Key figure in Minnesota food fraud receives 30-year prison sentence - CBS News

Thu, 29 Oct 2026 03:37:55 GMT

Salim Said, a key figure in a significant food fraud scheme in Minnesota, has been sentenced to 30 years in prison for defrauding a federal food program during the COVID-19 pandemic. Said, who operated Safari Restaurant in Minneapolis, was found guilty of falsely claiming to have fed thousands of needy children, while actual meal distributions were drastically overstated. Prosecutors revealed that he submitted fraudulent reimbursement claims for 21 consecutive months, amounting to $44 million in fraud, which included fictitious children. His actions, alongside those of Aimee Bock, the leader of the nonprofit Feeding Our Future, drew national attention, including criticism from former President Donald Trump. Bock is currently serving a 42-year sentence for orchestrating the scheme. The Minnesota Department of Education had received multiple complaints about the nonprofit but failed to take adequate action. The case has raised concerns about oversight in federal food assistance programs.
