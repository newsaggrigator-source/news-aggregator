Somalia: Disease outbreaks surge after floods | Health services now seriously strained - The Tanzania Times

Wed, 11 Nov 2026 20:12:33 GMT

In Northern Somalia, severe flooding has led to a surge in disease outbreaks, straining health services in the region. The United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) has reported rising cases of cholera and acute watery diarrhea, with five confirmed cholera cases, including one death, in Eastern Gaalkacyo. Since the beginning of the year, over 5,800 cases of acute watery diarrhea have been documented, primarily due to contaminated water sources and overflowing latrines. Additionally, Hirshabelle State has seen over 1,800 suspected measles cases and 102 suspected diphtheria cases. The World Health Organization and UN partners are mobilizing resources, deploying medical teams, and enhancing disease surveillance. However, health facilities are overwhelmed and under-resourced, with less than 40% of the required funding for the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan received. The ongoing flooding, driven by El Niño, has affected over 175,000 people, with projections of 874,000 displaced due to rising water levels.
