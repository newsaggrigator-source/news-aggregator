Giving Power Back to Society: Messay Kebede’s Challenge and the Ethiopian Iddir - Borkena

Tue, 24 Nov 2026 17:21:35 GMT

In his review of Professor Messay Kebede's book, "Ethiopian Modernization: Opportunities and Derailments," Tegegne Tefera explores the concept of Iddir, a traditional Ethiopian community association that plays a crucial role in local society. Iddirs manage funeral arrangements and provide financial support to bereaved families through member contributions, operating independently of government oversight. Kebede argues that Ethiopia's modernization efforts have repeatedly failed due to centralized power that stifles organized society and perpetuates conflict. He advocates for reform that redistributes power back to communities, transforming citizens from passive subjects into active participants. Tefera suggests that Iddirs could serve as a model for this change, as they are self-funded, democratically governed, and inclusive across ethnic and social lines. With over 7,800 Iddirs in Addis Ababa alone, their widespread presence and adaptability highlight their potential to foster a more participatory society, challenging the traditional state-centric power dynamics in Ethiopia.
