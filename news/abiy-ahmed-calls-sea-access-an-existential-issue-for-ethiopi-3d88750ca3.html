Abiy Ahmed Calls Sea Access an Existential Issue for Ethiopia’s 130 Million People - FTL Somalia

Sun, 25 Oct 2026 07:31:21 GMT

Ethiopian Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has emphasized that access to the sea is a critical issue for Ethiopia's 130 million people, linking it to national security and economic growth. Since losing its coastline to Eritrea in 1993, Ethiopia has depended on Djibouti for over 90% of its trade, which Abiy argues limits the country's economic potential. He has framed the sea access issue as existential, questioning the historical decisions that led to Ethiopia's landlocked status. In January 2024, Ethiopia signed a controversial agreement with Somaliland for access to the port of Berbera, which Somalia condemned as a violation of its sovereignty. The situation escalated tensions with Eritrea, which accused Ethiopia of aggressive intentions. Although a recent Ankara Declaration aimed to ease relations between Somalia and Ethiopia, the underlying issues remain unresolved. Abiy's insistence on peaceful negotiations contrasts with the complex diplomatic landscape, raising questions about Ethiopia's long-term strategy for securing maritime access.
