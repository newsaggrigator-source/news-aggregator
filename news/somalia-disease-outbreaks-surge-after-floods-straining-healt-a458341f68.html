Somalia: Disease outbreaks surge after floods, straining health services - Africa.com

Wed, 11 Nov 2026 20:45:27 GMT

Following severe flooding in Somalia's Mudug region, health services are under significant strain due to a surge in disease outbreaks. The UN's Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) reports rising cases of suspected cholera and acute watery diarrhoea, with five confirmed cholera cases, including one fatality, in Gaalkacyo. Since January, over 5,800 acute watery diarrhoea cases have been documented, exacerbated by contaminated water and overflowing latrines. Additionally, Hirshabelle State has seen more than 1,800 suspected measles cases and 102 suspected diphtheria cases. Contributing factors include low immunization rates and limited healthcare access. In response, the World Health Organization and humanitarian partners are deploying mobile medical teams and enhancing disease surveillance, but health facilities are overwhelmed and under-resourced. Urgent funding is needed, as less than 40% of the required $852 million for the 2026 Humanitarian Response Plan has been secured, with health funding falling short of one-third of its target.
