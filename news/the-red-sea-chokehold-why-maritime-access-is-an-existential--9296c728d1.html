The Red Sea Chokehold: Why Maritime Access Is an Existential Question for Ethiopia - horn review

Wed, 04 Nov 2026 12:07:40 GMT

Ethiopia's quest for maritime access is a critical issue, transcending mere economic considerations to encompass national security and survival. As the most populous landlocked nation, Ethiopia relies heavily on Djibouti for 95% of its trade, making it vulnerable to external political influences and potential economic collapse. The Red Sea has become a militarized zone, with various global powers establishing military bases, complicating Ethiopia's situation further. Recent alliances among Egypt, Eritrea, and Somalia aim to isolate Ethiopia, denying it a voice in regional maritime governance. This encirclement, coupled with tensions over the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, exacerbates Ethiopia's fears of being cut off from essential resources. The country views securing a reliable maritime corridor as a legitimate right, essential for its sovereignty and economic future. Ethiopia advocates for cooperative regional dialogue to address its concerns while emphasizing the need for international support to prevent escalating tensions into conflict.
