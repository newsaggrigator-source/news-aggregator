The World I Taught, the War I Witnessed - a Personal Reckoning With the Erosion of International Law in Tigray

Tue, 10 Nov 2026 16:09:11 GMT

In a reflective piece, an academic from Mekelle University recounts his experiences during the Tigray conflict (2020-2022) and the erosion of international law. He highlights the stark contrast between the principles of International Humanitarian Law (IHL), which he taught for over two decades, and the brutal realities faced by civilians in conflict zones like Tigray, Ukraine, and Gaza. Despite the establishment of legal frameworks designed to protect human rights during warfare, the article argues that these laws are often ignored, leading to widespread atrocities, including starvation and violence against civilians. The author shares personal grief over the loss of loved ones during the war and his role in documenting human rights abuses. He emphasizes the need for genuine accountability and the enforcement of IHL, criticizing the international community's selective application of these laws. The piece serves as a call to prioritize human rights over geopolitical interests to restore the rule of law and protect vulnerable populations.
