War In Amhara, Ethiopia Is Threatening Water Security In The Nile Basin – Analysis - Eurasia Review

Mon, 23 Nov 2026 02:04:33 GMT

The ongoing conflict in Ethiopia's Amhara region, now in its fourth year, poses a significant threat to water security in the Nile Basin. The war, characterized by the use of drones and heavy artillery, has devastated churches and monasteries that historically protect the surrounding ecosystems, including Lake Tana, which is crucial for the Blue Nile's flow. This disruption endangers agricultural practices and watershed management, risking severe ecological damage and diminishing water availability for Ethiopia, Sudan, and Egypt. The article emphasizes the need for international attention, urging an immediate cessation of attacks on populated areas and sacred sites, alongside humanitarian access and inclusive political dialogue. It warns that ignoring the conflict could exacerbate regional tensions over water resources, particularly concerning the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam. Sustainable peace in Amhara is essential for long-term water security across the Nile Basin, highlighting the interconnectedness of local stability and broader regional dynamics.
