The article explores the evolving relationship between Sudan and Ethiopia through the lens of Sudanese military leader Abdel Fattah al-Burhan. Since assuming leadership in 2019, Burhan's personal experiences and military background have significantly influenced Sudan's approach to Ethiopia, particularly regarding the disputed Al-Fashaga territory. The confrontation over this area intensified following Ethiopia's internal conflict in 2020, allowing Sudan to assert control and displace Ethiopian farmers. Burhan's actions have been framed as a nationalist endeavor, bolstering his political legitimacy amid Sudan's fragile civil-military transition. The article also highlights the broader regional dynamics, including Sudan's military cooperation with Egypt and the implications of the ongoing Sudanese civil war, which have intertwined with Ethiopia's security concerns. Despite attempts at diplomatic engagement, the relationship remains strained, with both nations navigating complex territorial and political challenges that threaten regional stability. The convergence of military and political interests complicates the path toward peace. (160 words)
