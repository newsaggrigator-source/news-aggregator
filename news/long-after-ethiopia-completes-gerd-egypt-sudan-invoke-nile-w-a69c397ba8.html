Long After Ethiopia Completes GERD, Egypt, Sudan Invoke Nile Water Rights Under Colonial-Era Agreement

Tue, 03 Nov 2026 15:18:58 GMT

Egypt and Sudan have reiterated their opposition to Ethiopia's management of the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD), invoking colonial-era water-sharing agreements to assert their claims. Following a recent "2+2" consultation in Cairo, the two countries issued a joint statement emphasizing their water security as "indivisible" and warning that Ethiopia's unilateral actions threaten downstream water availability. They called for a binding legal framework and negotiations based on international law, rejecting any involvement from other Nile Basin nations. Ethiopia, which inaugurated the GERD in September 2025, argues that the dam is crucial for its development and does not infringe on the water rights of downstream countries. The historical 1959 Nile Waters Agreement, which allocated water without Ethiopia's participation, is contested by Ethiopia, which insists on equitable use of shared resources. Tensions have escalated as Egypt and Sudan seek to strengthen their diplomatic coordination against Ethiopia's development plans.
