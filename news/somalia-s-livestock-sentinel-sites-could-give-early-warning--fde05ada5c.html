Somalia’s Livestock Sentinel Sites Could Give Early Warning for Three Zoonotic Viruses - Bioengineer.org

Wed, 28 Oct 2026 22:37:30 GMT

Researchers propose that Somalia's Livestock Sentinel Sites could serve as early-warning systems for three zoonotic viruses: Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV), Rift Valley fever virus (RVFV), and Crimean–Congo haemorrhagic fever virus (CCHFV). These sites, where humans frequently interact with livestock, could effectively monitor animal, vector, and human health signals together. The authors emphasize the importance of targeted surveillance, particularly in high-volume livestock markets and export quarantine stations. They suggest specific monitoring strategies for each virus, including camel respiratory sampling for MERS-CoV and tick surveillance for CCHFV. The proposal aims to enhance Somalia's zoonotic disease preparedness by detecting potential spillover events before they escalate into severe human health crises. The authors acknowledge varying levels of evidence for each virus but argue that a coordinated One Health approach is crucial for effective surveillance and response. This framework is based on regional studies and aims to strengthen Somalia's capacity to manage zoonotic threats. (157 words)
