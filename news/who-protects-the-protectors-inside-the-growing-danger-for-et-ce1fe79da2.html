Who Protects the Protectors? Inside the Growing Danger for Ethiopia's Humanitarian Workers

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 05:14:50 GMT

In Ethiopia, humanitarian workers face escalating dangers, including killings, kidnappings, and threats, as they strive to assist those affected by hunger, displacement, and conflict. Recent incidents highlight the urgent need for better protection systems for these workers. For instance, Mahelet Sitotaw Abera was fatally injured in crossfire while collecting humanitarian data, and Yared Melese was abducted for ransom, later confirmed dead. The UN has condemned these attacks, emphasizing the necessity for accountability and protection under international humanitarian law. The situation is exacerbated by armed groups seizing humanitarian vehicles and the use of drones, which pose new risks to aid operations. Reports indicate a significant increase in violence against aid workers globally, with hundreds affected in 2025 alone. The article stresses that protecting humanitarian personnel is crucial not only for their safety but also for the communities relying on their assistance. Effective measures must be implemented to ensure their safety and uphold their vital role in crisis response.
