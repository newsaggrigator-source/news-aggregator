The El Alamein–Aswan Axis and the Strategic Containment of Ethiopia - horn review

Mon, 23 Nov 2026 19:52:13 GMT

The recent meeting in Aswan among the foreign ministers of Egypt, Sudan, and Eritrea marks a significant step in regional alignment aimed at containing Ethiopia's influence. This trilateral consultation, which followed a prior summit in El Alamein, emphasized the governance of the Red Sea by its coastal states and reaffirmed support for Sudan's sovereignty and Somalia's territorial integrity. The discussions reflect a coordinated diplomatic effort to address shared concerns, particularly regarding the Nile and Red Sea security, amidst rising tensions involving Ethiopia. Ethiopia's recent diplomatic rupture with Eritrea and its ongoing internal conflicts further complicate the situation. The joint statements from these meetings highlight a commitment to prior notification and consultation over transboundary water issues, particularly concerning the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD). However, the legal basis for these agreements raises questions, as Ethiopia was not part of the 1959 Nile Waters Agreement, which Egypt and Sudan reference to assert their claims. International water law principles advocate for equitable utilization and cooperation among all riparian states.
