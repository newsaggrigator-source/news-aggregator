Security In The Red Sea and The Case for an Inclusive Maritime Order - horn review

Mon, 02 Nov 2026 18:06:47 GMT

The Red Sea and Gulf of Aden are crucial for global trade, linking the Mediterranean and Indian Ocean, and their security is vital for the Horn of Africa's stability. Disruptions in this region, whether from piracy or geopolitical tensions, have far-reaching impacts. The article argues against the notion that governance of the Red Sea should be limited to coastal states, emphasizing that Ethiopia, as a landlocked nation, has legitimate maritime interests due to its economic reliance on maritime access. It advocates for an inclusive maritime security framework that respects coastal sovereignty while allowing landlocked states to participate in regional security discussions. The United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea supports this approach, promoting cooperation among bordering states. Historical precedents show that landlocked countries can secure maritime access through negotiated agreements. Ethiopia's maritime strategy should focus on building partnerships with coastal states to enhance security and economic cooperation, ensuring mutual benefits and regional stability.
