Why Ethiopia Has Not Found Peace — and How Ethiopians Can Build It - Borkena

Sat, 21 Nov 2026 05:19:28 GMT

Ethiopia's ongoing conflict, particularly since Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's rise to power in 2018, reveals deep-rooted issues surrounding political legitimacy and governance. Initially celebrated for reforms and a peace prize for rapprochement with Eritrea, Abiy's administration has systematically dismantled national institutions, favoring Oromo elites and exacerbating ethnic tensions. The resultant violence, particularly against Amhara and Tigrayan communities, has led to accusations of war crimes and ethnic cleansing. Despite the 2022 Pretoria agreement halting major hostilities, underlying disputes remain unresolved, with significant stakeholders excluded from negotiations. The government's intolerance of dissent and reliance on division further complicate peace efforts. To achieve lasting stability, a comprehensive peace process must include accountability, inclusive negotiations, and protections for civilians. Immediate actions should focus on protecting lives, facilitating humanitarian access, and initiating dialogue among all affected parties. Without addressing these foundational issues, Ethiopia's path to peace remains elusive, with the potential for broader regional conflict looming.
