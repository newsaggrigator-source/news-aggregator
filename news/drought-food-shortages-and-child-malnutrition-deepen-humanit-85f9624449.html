Drought, Food Shortages and Child Malnutrition Deepen Humanitarian Needs in Afar, Amhara and Tigray

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 10:25:19 GMT

A recent humanitarian assessment highlights the escalating crisis in Ethiopia's Afar, Amhara, and Tigray regions, exacerbated by severe drought, food shortages, and rising child malnutrition rates. The report, dated October 19, reveals that approximately 15% of 11,800 screened children in internally displaced communities in Tigray suffer from moderate acute malnutrition. The ongoing conflict and El Niño-related drought have compounded existing vulnerabilities, leading to significant food insecurity, particularly in southern Tigray, where crop failures have devastated agricultural production. Health services are under severe strain due to insecurity, shortages of medical supplies, and unpaid salaries, causing health workers to leave. Displacement remains a critical issue, with many civilians moving towards Shire amid ongoing hostilities. The assessment emphasizes the urgent need for improved humanitarian access and rapid needs assessments to address the fragmented information and guide responses effectively. Despite some improvements in access, challenges persist, complicating the humanitarian situation further.
