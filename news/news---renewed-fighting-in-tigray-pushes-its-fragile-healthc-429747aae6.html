News - Renewed Fighting in Tigray Pushes Its Fragile Healthcare System Closer to Collapse, Again

Wed, 11 Nov 2026 10:36:14 GMT

Renewed fighting in Tigray, Ethiopia, has severely strained the region's already fragile healthcare system, pushing it closer to collapse. Hospitals in Mekelle have been overwhelmed by casualties from recent clashes between Ethiopian federal forces and Tigrayan fighters, which reignited a two-year cessation of hostilities. Medical staff report chaotic conditions, with emergency rooms flooded and resources stretched thin. The conflict has exacerbated existing shortages of healthcare workers, medicines, and ambulances, with many doctors going unpaid for months. The World Health Organization has warned that ongoing violence is further undermining healthcare access. Attacks on medical facilities have persisted, with reports of looting and armed takeovers, complicating the delivery of essential services. A healthcare worker noted over 100 maternal deaths in the past year due to a lack of resources. The situation reflects a deepening crisis, with the region's healthcare infrastructure still reeling from the devastating Tigray war of 2020-2022.
