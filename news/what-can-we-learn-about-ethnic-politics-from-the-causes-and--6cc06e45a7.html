What can we learn about ethnic politics from the causes and consequences of the current Moyale conflict? - Borkena

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 16:42:14 GMT

The ongoing conflict in Moyale, southern Ethiopia, highlights the complexities of ethnic politics and the consequences of historical land disputes. The clashes between the Borana (Oromo) and Somali communities stem from a century of colonial disruption and the strategic manipulation of ethnic identities by successive Ethiopian regimes. The establishment of ethnic federalism in 1991 by the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF) exacerbated these tensions, transforming historical grievances into systemic territorial conflicts. The conflict's roots trace back to the late 19th century, when Somali groups began encroaching on Borana lands, a situation worsened by colonial policies that favored certain ethnic groups. The EPRDF's demographic engineering, including the resettlement of Somali populations into contested areas, aimed to dilute Borana political power, demonstrating how regimes prioritize control over peace. This situation underscores the destructive potential of ethnic federalism and the sacrifices made for political survival, revealing the intricate relationship between ethnicity and governance in Ethiopia.
